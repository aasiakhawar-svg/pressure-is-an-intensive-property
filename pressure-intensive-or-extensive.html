Why is pressure an intensive property?

Dalton’s law adds pressures, so is pressure extensive? Watch the particles, do the sums, and the confusion disappears.

✍️ Prepared by Aasia Khawar

1 · The one test that decides

To test a property, make an exact copy of the whole system, container included, and look at the property of the combined system.

Extensivedoubles when you double the system. Examples: mass, volume, moles of gas, total energy.
Intensivestays the same. Examples: temperature, density, pressure.
⚠️ Common mistakeSqueezing more particles into the same box is not doubling the system. It changes the system. That is why more particles gives more pressure.

2 · Watch it happen

Pick a scene. Each blue or pink dot is a gas particle bouncing off the walls. The readings use P = nRT/V with T = 300 K, R = 0.0821 L·atm/(mol·K) and 20 particles = 0.0406 mol.

3 · The calculations, step by step

A · One box (scene ①)

n = 0.0406 mol, V = 1.00 L, T = 300 K
P = nRT ÷ V = (0.0406 × 0.0821 × 300) ÷ 1.00
P = 1.00 atm

B · Copy the whole system (scene ②)

n = 2 × 0.0406 = 0.0812 mol ← doubled
V = 2 × 1.00 = 2.00 L ← doubled
P = (0.0812 × 0.0821 × 300) ÷ 2.00 = 2.00 ÷ 2.00 = 1.00 atm
n and V both ×2 → P × 1 → intensive

C · Add gas B to the same box (Dalton’s law, scene ③)

P(A) = 1.00 atm (as before)
P(B) = (0.0406 × 0.0821 × 300) ÷ 1.00 = 1.00 atm
P(total) = P(A) + P(B) = 1.00 + 1.00 = 2.00 atm
Check: (0.0812 × 0.0821 × 300) ÷ 1.00 = 2.00 atm ✔
What Dalton addsDalton adds different gases sharing one space, each with its own partial pressure. It never adds a gas to a copy of itself.

D · Two boxes, each with A + B (scene ④)

each box: P = (0.0812 × 0.0821 × 300) ÷ 1.00 = 2.00 atm
both boxes together: n = 0.1624 mol, V = 2.00 L
P = (0.1624 × 0.0821 × 300) ÷ 2.00 = 4.00 ÷ 2.00 = 2.00 atm (not 4 atm)

4 · Remember it with a classroom 🏫

Extensive = how manyStudents in total: two classrooms of 30 hold 60.
Intensive = how crowdedEach room is still just as crowded. Pressure is “crowdedness” of particles, hitting each patch of wall.

Adding 30 more students to one room makes it more crowded. That is scene ③, and it is why Dalton’s law raises the pressure.

🎯 Exam answer in one linePressure is intensive because P = nRT/V depends on n/V, so copying the whole system doubles n and V and leaves P unchanged. Dalton’s law adds the partial pressures of different gases in the same volume; it does not make pressure scale with the amount of material.
